ECX · the eCash fork · block 973,768

Coins without keys

At its fork, eCash moved 506,352 coins out of 10,127 outputs that had sat untouched since 2009, the coins commonly attributed to Satoshi, with 232 transactions that nobody signed. Here is what happened, why it breaks the one promise every fork makes, and why the XBT community, and its exchanges, should want nothing to do with it.

01 · WHAT HAPPENED

A ledger rule that says: these coins don't need their key

eCash (ticker ECX) is a hard fork of Bitcoin's SHA256d chain, led by Paul Sztorc of LayerTwo Labs, that went live at block 973,728. Everyone holding bitcoin at the snapshot got one ECX per BTC, controlled by the same keys. That part is an ordinary fork.

Then there is src/repo_txns.h in eCash's own node. It is a hard-coded list of 232 transaction IDs that the node calls repurpose transactions: their inputs are not checked for signatures. Any transaction on that list is valid without the owner's key.

All 232 were mined in ECX block 973,768, forty blocks after the fork. Between them they spend 10,127 inputs. Every input is a 50-coin coinbase output from 2009, paid to a bare public key, with an empty signature. The ones we sampled are from May 2009 and are still unspent on Bitcoin today. Their owner has not moved them in seventeen years. On ECX, they were moved for them.

On ECX, owning the key was not enough to keep the coins.

The press reported "about 500,000 coins to investors". The chain says 506,352, to 233 addresses. eCash's own description is that roughly 600,000 stay with Satoshi's addresses and the rest go to investors who fund the project before launch.

02 · "BUT NO BITCOIN MOVED"

True, and beside the point

eCash's defence is accurate as far as it goes. Paul Sztorc:

We do not take any of Satoshi's BTC. We gift Satoshi 600,000 eCash … BTC balances are untouched by eCash. (via The Block)

Nobody can move a satoshi on Bitcoin without the key, and eCash didn't. But think about what ECX is. A fork has nothing to offer except one promise: whatever your keys controlled before, they control after. That promise is the whole reason anyone values the new coin. It is why you get ECX at all.

ECX makes that promise to everyone, then breaks it for one holder. And it breaks it in the most self-serving direction possible, toward the people who paid for the fork. "We gift Satoshi 600,000" is a strange way to describe keeping 506,352 of someone else's coins for yourself. You cannot gift someone what was already theirs.

A ledger that honours keys for everyone except the people its developers choose doesn't have property rights. It has permissions.

03 · WHO DECIDES WHAT'S ABANDONED?

The precedent is the product

The argument for taking these coins is that they look abandoned. They haven't moved in more than a decade, so surely nobody will mind.

Bitcoin's answer has always been that this is exactly the question nobody gets to ask. Coins in deep cold storage look abandoned. An inheritance waiting on a lawyer looks abandoned. A long-term holder who simply doesn't trade looks abandoned. The moment a chain's developers can decide that someone's coins are "unused", and move them by consensus to people who funded the work, every balance on that chain is provisional. It is held at the developers' pleasure.

There is a second reason these coins are different. Whoever holds them has left them untouched for seventeen years: a founder who never cashed in, and whose untouched coins have quietly benefited every holder since. Whether the keys were destroyed, lost, or simply never used, the effect is the same, and it is the clearest act of restraint in Bitcoin's history. ECX didn't just move dormant coins. It overrode that choice and handed the result to the people who paid for the fork.

That is why reasonable people across the spectrum objected, often people who agree on nothing else. Vijay Selvam: sets a precedent that irreparably damages Bitcoin's monetary properties. Beau Turner: Bitcoin was created to preserve and protect inviolable property rights for everybody on earth (both via CoinDesk). Even CoinDesk's own write-up conceded that it "reads as theft, even when no theft is technically occurring."

04 · WHO GOT THEM

Not spread out. Concentrated.

The 506,352 coins went to 233 addresses, but not evenly:

Top 10 addresses: 46% Next 40: 37% Other 183: 17%

The largest single address received 42,000 ECX. 87 addresses received a thousand or more. The chain cannot tell us who these people are. eCash tells us they are the investors who funded the fork. Either way, this is a pre-mine wearing a fork's clothes: a block of coins created for insiders, except the coins weren't created. They were taken from outputs whose owner never signed.

05 · WHAT XBT STANDS FOR

Rules, not rulers

XBT came out of a fork too, so it's fair to ask what makes it different. The BLAKE2b fork changed how blocks are mined. It did not change who owns what. Every balance that existed at the split carried across. Replay protection is opt-in, per signature, so each holder decides when and how to split their own coins. Nobody's coins were moved for them.

XBT's fork

  • Changed proof-of-work to BLAKE2b
  • Kept every balance as it was
  • Your key is the only way to move your coins
  • Replay protection you opt into, signature by signature

ECX's fork

  • Added Drivechains
  • Kept every balance, except the ones it chose
  • 232 transactions valid without any key
  • 506,352 coins to 233 addresses its backers say are investors

XBT's whole case to the world is that it is the honest continuation: same coins, same owners, same rules about keys, better resistance to the mining concentration that threatened the old chain. Everything that makes that case credible is the thing ECX threw away. Being associated with ECX doesn't just look bad for XBT. It undercuts the argument XBT exists to make.

06 · WHY A LISTING ISN'T NEUTRAL

The market is the point

Reassigned coins are only worth something if they can be sold. 506,352 ECX sitting in 233 addresses is a number on a ledger. 506,352 ECX with a spot market against USDT is money. The exchange that provides that market is the step where the coins taken at the fork turn into dollars.

As of 3 November 2026, ecash.com/buy lists exactly one spot market for ECX. It is first on the page, and it is an XBT exchange. The other two entries, CoinW and GMO Coin, are listed only for distributing ECX to BTC holders.

When the project's own buy page sends people to you, you are not a bystander to the project. You are its exit.

That matters more for an exchange that is central to XBT. Its XBT market is where much of the community trades; wallets pull their XBT price from it. Whatever it lists, it lends some of XBT's credibility to.

07 · THE OBJECTIONS

And why they don't hold

People want to trade it. Exchanges aren't moral police.

Most coins an exchange lists are speculative, many are silly, some are scams that hurt only the people who buy them. ECX is different in kind: its supply includes coins moved from someone who never consented, and the market is how the recipients realise them. The bar isn't "is this a good coin". It is "does listing this help someone cash out coins they took without a key". That bar is low, and ECX doesn't clear it.

It lets ECX holders swap into XBT. That's good for XBT.

Holders who received ECX the honest way, one per BTC under their own keys, can sell it on any venue that lists it. XBT gains very little from being the first on-ramp, and it is staking its reputation on a coin whose distribution contradicts everything XBT claims to be.

Satoshi isn't coming back. Nobody is actually harmed.

Nobody knows that, and that is exactly the point: the chain's developers decided it for him. If dormancy is a reason to move coins, every quiet holder is next in line. And the 233 recipients are harmed by nothing. They simply received half a million coins.

Someone paid for the listing.

Then the listing is a service sold to the project. That makes the question sharper, not softer.

08 · THE ASK

To the exchange: please delist ECX

You have done a great deal for XBT, and that is exactly why this matters. We're not asking you to police every coin. We are asking you not to be the market for this one.

  1. Delist ECX/USDT. Give holders notice and a withdrawal window, as you would for any delisting.
  2. Ask ecash.com to remove you from its buy page. Right now the project sends its buyers to you first.
  3. If you won't delist, at least put a plain warning on the market page, in your own words: that ECX's consensus rules moved 506,352 coins from 2009 outputs without signatures, to 233 addresses its backers describe as investors.

XBT's reputation is that it kept everyone's coins exactly where they were. Its exchanges are part of that reputation.

09 · CHECK IT YOURSELF

Don't take our word for it

Every number on this page comes from eCash's own code and eCash's own block explorer. Checked 3 November 2026.

The list of unsigned transactions, in eCash's node at tag ecash-v1.2.0-core32.1: src/repo_txns.h.

curl -s https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ecash-com/bitcoin/ecash-v1.2.0-core32.1/src/repo_txns.h \
  | grep -oE '[0-9a-f]{64}' | wc -l        # 232

One of them on eCash's explorer: inputs of 50 coins each, paid to bare public keys, with an empty scriptsig.

curl -s https://explorer.ecash.com/api/tx/db6dc9c1931c2887481a60e5e071e8de588712e7eb4c966f5a25262dfaad6460

The same coins are untouched on Bitcoin. This is the first input of the transaction above:

curl -s https://mempool.space/api/tx/999e1c837c76a1b7fbb7e57baf87b309960f5ffefbf2a9b95dd890602272f644/outspend/0
# {"spent":false}

Fetch all 232 and add them up and you get 10,127 inputs, every one unsigned, 506,352 coins in, 233 addresses out, all confirmed in ECX block 973,768.

Two things the chain cannot prove, and this page doesn't claim it does. That these outputs belong to Satoshi rests on the widely accepted "Patoshi" attribution, which has never been proven. That the 233 addresses belong to investors is eCash's own description.
